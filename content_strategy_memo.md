# Content Strategy Memo

**To:** Content Acquisition Team  **From:** Data Analytics  **Re:** What to greenlight next, based on a 9,515-title catalog snapshot (Netflix, Prime Video, Hulu, Disney+)

## Bottom line
Streaming catalogs have grown fast, but quality has not kept up and each platform has settled into a distinct audience position. The biggest opportunities are in **acclaimed titles** and **under-served age segments**, not in raw volume.

## Insight 1: Quality is the scarce resource, not volume
- Only **3.5%** of all titles score 80+ on Rotten Tomatoes. Average scores by release decade are *lower* for the 2010s (53) than for the 1990s (57), even though the 2010s hold 56% of the catalog.
- Prime Video has the largest library (4,113 titles) but the weakest ratings: average RT **50.4**, and just **1.3%** of titles at 80+. Disney+ reaches 8.1% on a library a quarter of the size.

**Recommendation:** Shift acquisition budget from bulk licensing toward a smaller number of 80+ titles. For a platform like Prime Video, each acclaimed title adds proportionally far more to catalog quality than another mid-tier package would.

## Insight 2: Family and teen content is an open lane outside Disney+
- Disney+ has **70%** of its catalog rated all/7+. Netflix (12.6%), Prime Video (14.1%) and Hulu (12.8%) are each in the low teens.
- Netflix is adult-heavy (24% 18+ plus 49% unrated), and Hulu is the most adult-rated catalog at 37% 18+.

**Recommendation:** Greenlight more 7+ and 13+ rated titles for Netflix and Prime Video. These are the platforms where family/teen supply is thinnest, competition outside Disney+ is light, and family viewing tends to support household retention.

## Insight 3: Fresh content differentiates; back catalog is a weak moat
- Netflix is a recency library: **86%** of titles are 2010+ and **48%** are 2018+. Prime Video (20% since 2018) and Disney+ (20%) lean on older titles, with 20-25% pre-1990.
- **97% of titles exist on only one platform**, so every acquisition is exclusive by default and the new-release pipeline is the main place platforms can pull ahead.
- Release counts fell sharply in 2020 on every platform except Disney+ (Netflix -95 titles vs 2019), a likely pandemic production effect that leaves room for those who can keep supply steady.

**Recommendation:** Prime Video and Disney+ should prioritize 2018+ acquisitions and originals to close the freshness gap, while keeping a curated set of classics (Disney+'s Mary Poppins-type titles score 96) as a retention asset rather than as bulk.

## Caveats
No genre, country or duration data was available, so genre and regional trends are not covered. Ratings are Rotten Tomatoes only (critic scores, not viewership). 44% of titles have no age rating and score lower on average (47 vs 55-61), so audience-mix figures should be read with that in mind. 2021 is a partial year. Findings describe catalog composition, not viewing behavior.
